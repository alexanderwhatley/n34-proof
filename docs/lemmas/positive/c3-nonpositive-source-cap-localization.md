# Nonpositive middle coefficient selects the first source bound

Read [the appendix terminology](../../appendix.md#terminology) before this technical lemma. Its variables are local to this document.

In the native upper-low setting l>1,0<a<b<xi,k>0, assume the derivative bound k<(1+2a)/[(b-a)(a+l)]. The linear-cubic support inequality gives C3<=0=>f3<0, where S=a+b+2h,s=l-h,J=2h(1-h).

The elementary expansion

f3=4S[ab-l+h(a+b+2l)+h(1-h)/2]+2Js

then gives

**C3<=0=>ab<l.**

In the original positive-source setting, the coefficient bounds R1>0 and R2>0 are

k[l(a+b)-ab]<1, k[a+b-l]<1.

For ab<l and l>1,

[l(a+b)-ab]-[a+b-l]=(l-1)(a+b)+l-ab>0.

Moreover l(a+b)-ab>0: since a+b>=2sqrt(ab) and sqrt(ab)<sqrt(l)<2l, one has l(a+b)-ab>=sqrt(ab)[2l-sqrt(ab)]>0. Consequently **the R1 scale ceiling is finite and strictly tighter than the R2 ceiling whenever C3<=0** in the positive-source upper-low region. There is no source-bound selection split left on this residual branch.

This is a source-bound localization, not an exclusion of the remaining alternating coefficient pattern. The coefficient-only implication ab<l retains its meaning without positive coefficients of R, but the source ceilings require R1,R2>0.
